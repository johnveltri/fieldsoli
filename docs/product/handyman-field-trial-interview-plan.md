# Handyman field trial: 30-minute interview plan

Status: exploring research plan. This document does not describe shipped product behavior or approved marketing copy.

## Purpose

Learn how an independent Chicago handyman actually records work, whether FieldSoli fits that workflow after a one-week trial on a few real jobs, and what would make them use it again. The participant receives $150 for giving the app an honest try and completing the conversation, including if they decide it is not a fit.

The interview should reconstruct specific jobs and observed actions. Avoid asking the participant to predict whether they would use or pay for a hypothetical feature.

## Before the call

- Ask the participant to have FieldSoli and one recent job available. They can hide customer names, addresses, amounts, and other private details when showing a screen.
- Review which trial features were actually available to them. Ask about estimates and invoices only if they could use those flows.
- Have a place to record exact phrases, actions, points of hesitation, and follow-up questions. Ask before recording audio or video.
- Explain that candid criticism is useful and payment does not depend on positive feedback.

## Interview guide

| Time | Goal | Questions and prompts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 min | Understand their existing process | “Before this week, walk me through your most recent job, from the first customer contact to getting paid. What did you record, where, and when?” “What information do you look up later?” |
| 5–15 min | Reconstruct the trial | “Tell me about the first job you put into FieldSoli. What made you open it at that moment?” “What did you enter later, skip, or record somewhere else?” “Was there a job this week you did not put in? Walk me through why.” |
| 15–22 min | Observe a real task | “Could you show me how you would add time or an expense from a recent job?” Let them drive. If they pause, ask, “What were you expecting to find?” If they cannot complete it, ask what they would do instead. |
| 22–27 min | Find the value and the alternative | “What, if anything, was useful enough that you would do it again on your next job?” “What would you use instead?” “Did seeing the job’s time, expenses, or earnings tell you anything you did not already know?” |
| 27–30 min | Capture the biggest issue | “What was the most frustrating part?” “If you stopped using FieldSoli tomorrow, what would be the reason?” “What should I have asked you?” |

If the participant created or tried to send an estimate or invoice, ask them to walk through one specific document: what they needed to send, what happened in FieldSoli, and whether they had to finish elsewhere. If they did not use the feature, ask whether it came up during the trial rather than assuming they missed it.

## How to ask

- Follow broad answers with “Which job was that?”, “Can you show me?”, “What happened next?”, or “Tell me more about that.”
- Let the participant finish a task before explaining the intended workflow. Record where their expectation differed from the app.
- Ask about the current alternative, including paper, memory, notes, spreadsheets, and other apps. Do not assume another software product is the main competitor.
- Keep the time boxes flexible when a specific story reveals something important.

## After each call

Write a short summary with:

1. The specific job or task the participant was trying to complete.
2. What they did in FieldSoli, including any skipped or abandoned steps.
3. The moment FieldSoli helped or got in the way, with their own words where possible.
4. What they used instead and whether they chose FieldSoli again on another job.
5. One open question to test in the next interview.

Keep observed behavior separate from the founder’s interpretation. Compare patterns across participants before changing the product or public pitch.

## Research basis

- [Bob Moesta on Jobs to Be Done](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xQV7HVyAJjc): reconstruct the context and sequence behind a real choice; follow the participant’s story with concrete examples.
- [Gustaf Alströmer on talking to users](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/summary-lessons-from-working-with-600-yc-startups--gustaf-alströmer-y-combinator): observe people using the product to see where they struggle.
- [Todd Jackson on validating a startup idea](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/validating-your-startup-idea): learn from actual customer experiences and test an early product with real users.
