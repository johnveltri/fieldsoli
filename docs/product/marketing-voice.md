# FieldSoli marketing voice

Owner-approved guidance, October 26, 2026. Applies to store listings, screenshot headlines, website copy, campaigns, and other public-facing marketing.

## Voice

Write for experienced tradespeople. Use short, direct, factual language. State what the product does and the concrete differences it offers. Let the product and its actual screens demonstrate the value.

Respect the reader's judgment and experience. Avoid motivational coaching, forced audience slogans, and language that sounds like instruction on how to run their business. Do not turn every feature into a promise about improving the reader's decisions or income.

## Preferred examples

- Track time & expenses
- Unlimited invoices & estimates
- Simple. Fast.
- Take your data with you.

The selected supporting line for export is “CSV export. No active subscription required.” This is a product-access claim, not a generic slogan: verify that export remains available without an active subscription before publication. It does not promise a complete account backup or lifetime access to every feature.

## Avoid

| Rejected wording | Why it does not fit | Preferred approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Know your profit | Tacky and potentially condescending; implies the reader needs coaching | Show actual earnings, costs, and net/hour with factual labels |
| Price the next one smarter / Price your next one smarter | Tacky and potentially condescending; frames the reader's judgment as needing improvement | Describe job history and comparisons without a pricing lesson |
| Built for solo | Forced audience slogan | Use contractor, handyman, or independent tradesperson when identifying the audience is useful |

Avoid close slogan-like paraphrases of these lines, not just the exact words. This does not ban the word “solo”: audience descriptions such as the approved store subtitle “Unlimited for Solo Contractors” remain appropriate.

## Brand strategy and discovery

The [brand strategy](brand-strategy.md) guides product direction. Learning from job outcomes remains part of that strategy, but it does not need to become an instructional headline. Show recorded time, expenses, earnings, and completed-job comparisons as information the reader can use on their own terms.

Keep discovery language explicit: estimates, invoices, contractors, and handymen where relevant. Profit, earnings, time, costs, and net/hour are valid descriptive metrics and search terms. The objection is the patronizing tone of a slogan, not the underlying capability.

Describe differences through supported FieldSoli capabilities. Keep competitor names, tier comparisons, and evidence in research; do not imply a competitor lacks a feature when it is available on another plan. Support “Simple. Fast.” with an actual focused interaction rather than extra adjectives or invented speed claims.

## Copy review

- Does the line state a capability or a concrete, supported difference?
- Does it respect the reader's existing skill and judgment?
- Can the product demonstrate it without an additional coaching slogan?
- Are capacity, access, and subscription claims accurate for the release?

If a line could sound like a lesson about running a trade business, rewrite it as a factual feature statement.
