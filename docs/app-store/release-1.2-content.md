# FieldSoli 1.2: Discovery-First App Store Content

Status: Proposed launch content; not published or evidence of release readiness.
Research reviewed: October 25, 2026. Marketing voice established: October 26, 2026. Selected creative and copy updated: October 31, 2026.
Market: United States, English (US). Platforms: iOS and Android.

## Objective and launch decisions

Help US contractors searching for estimating and invoicing apps discover FieldSoli. Relevant discovery comes first; brand differentiation and page conversion support it. Use functional language in searchable metadata, then show why FieldSoli fits someone working solo.

- Primary audience: solo residential handymen. Broader discovery audience: independent contractors and tradespeople.
- Primary capabilities: create and share estimate and invoice documents using prices entered for a job.
- Supporting value: unlimited jobs and documents, simple field capture, and learning from recorded time, costs, and earnings.
- Launch assumption confirmed by the product owner: jobs, estimates, and invoices have no count limits and will be available to all new users in 1.2. Confirm actual access separately on each released platform before publishing.
- Deliverables: this content plan, repository marketing voice guidance, and the owner-selected images in [final-images](release-1.2/final-images/README.md). No store publication, application changes, or paid acquisition is included.

**Public-facing message:** Simple estimates and invoices. Unlimited jobs and documents. Track time & expenses. Take your data with you.

### Marketing voice

Repository-wide source: [FieldSoli marketing voice](../product/marketing-voice.md). Apply that guide to future marketing work as well as this release.

Use direct, factual language that respects experienced tradespeople. State the capability or concrete difference and let the product demonstrate its value. Keep copy short and matter-of-fact; avoid forced audience slogans, motivational coaching, or language that implies the reader needs to be taught how to run their business.

The owner rejected “know your profit” and “price the next one smarter” as tacky and potentially condescending, and “built for solo” as forced. Do not reuse those lines or close paraphrases in launch copy. “Simple. Fast.” and specific statements such as “Track time & expenses” match the intended voice. Descriptive terms such as earnings, profit, and net/hour remain appropriate for actual metrics and relevant search terms.

Learning from job outcomes remains part of the internal brand strategy. Show time, costs, earnings, and completed-job comparisons without turning that strategy into advice-like headlines. Retain contractor and handyman terms where they serve discovery; the approved subtitle remains unchanged.

### Interpretation for FieldSoli

- **Shared category vocabulary:** all three competitors make estimating explicit in their names. Joist and Contractor+ also make invoicing explicit. This supports functional title placement, but does not establish which query has the highest volume.
- **Closest document-workflow benchmark:** Joist joins contractor intent with estimates, invoices, and organization. Organization alone is insufficient differentiation.
- **Breadth benchmark:** Contractor+ promotes extensive operations and job costing. FieldSoli should demonstrate its focused solo experience without claiming unique profit tracking or judging a competitor's usability without testing it.
- **Different estimating intent:** SimplyWise strongly promotes calculating prices with AI. FieldSoli's current capability is preparing documents from entered prices and learning from actual outcomes. Avoid attracting users expecting photo-to-price automation.
- **Conversion story:** show documents, unlimited capacity, time and expense tracking, and CSV export. Present earnings and comparisons as available information, without coaching the reader about pricing. Do not claim the combination is exclusive or guarantee earnings.

### Platform rules and rationale

- **iOS:** put the two core functions in the name, retain contractor audience in the subtitle, and use the keyword field for complementary relevant terms. The proposed ASCII keyword field has no repeated tokens and does not repeat name/subtitle words. Do not add singular/plural variants merely to fill space. Query coverage and stemming are hypotheses, not ranking guarantees. Apple limits names/subtitles to 30 characters and keywords to 100; the reference also specifies a 100-byte keyword allowance, so ASCII satisfies both. Promotional text supports conversion and does not affect search ranking. Description and release notes explain the product; they are not substitutes for searchable metadata. [Apple product-page guidance](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/) and [version-field reference](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/platform-version-information).
- **Google Play:** there is no equivalent hidden keyword field in this package. Use a natural title, short description, and full description to communicate function and relevance; Google does not publish exact ranking weights. Limits are 30, 80, and 4,000 characters respectively. Avoid repetitive keyword lists. Release notes and screenshot captions support understanding rather than replace those descriptive fields. [Play setup and limits](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859152?hl=en) and [listing guidance](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13393723?hl=en).
- **Tradeoff:** retain Unlimited for Solo Contractors in the iOS subtitle while putting both core functions in the name. “Simple” is explicit in the Play short description and iOS conversion copy. This preserves the accepted capacity-based positioning without displacing estimate/invoice discovery terms. It is a starting hypothesis, not a proven optimal allocation.

## Four-week discovery measurement plan

### Baseline and definitions

Before publication, save the preceding 28 complete days for US traffic where available. Record platform, date range, report definition, source filter, new versus returning users, live metadata, release events, and any paid campaigns. Use comparable seven-day windows after publication; compare weekly rates with baseline weekly averages, not a seven-day total with a 28-day total. If baseline is too small, report raw counts and uncertainty rather than a meaningful percentage lift.

Primary outcome: growth in **relevant non-branded discovery**. Where query-level reporting is available, separate FieldSoli brand queries from contractor/handyman/document queries and unrelated intent. Supporting outcomes: search exposure, listing visits, and first-time downloads attributable to available discovery sources. Track conversion alongside reach; traffic growth alone is insufficient.

| Platform | Available measures to collect | Limits and interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| iOS | US App Store Search unique impressions, product-page views, first-time/total downloads, and the console's conversion metric where available | Search source includes Apple Ads. No paid activity is planned; still record whether any exists and do not label mixed data organic. Standard source reporting does not establish organic query-level or brand/non-brand attribution. Downloads can occur from search without a page visit, so do not divide all search downloads by page visitors as a page-conversion rate. |
| Android | Available search terms, listing visitors, install-button clicks and console conversion, plus separately reported actual new-user acquisitions/downloads | Current Play listing reports emphasize clicks, not completed installs. Use the console's current source definitions: generic category searches can fall under Explore; inspect relevant terms across available sources. Suppressed/Other queries remain unknown. Do not equate clicks with installs or all Explore traffic with keyword search. |

Sources: [Apple acquisition definitions](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect-analytics/acquisition/acquisition) and [Google listing-performance definitions](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859173?hl=en). Keep the reporting limits with exported results, not just in this plan.

### Weekly actions

| Window | Review | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Confirm live metadata, access to documents, reporting availability, and any obviously mismatched search intent | Correct factual or metadata errors immediately. Record changes; do not claim early ranking gains from release timing alone. |
| Week 2 | Compare relevant query visits where visible, search exposure, download counts, and matched conversion measures | Document emerging intent. Keep launch metadata stable unless evidence shows a material relevance problem. |
| Week 3 | Check whether exposure gains also bring relevant visitors and downloads; compare with baseline and prior week | Separate discovery problems from conversion problems. Do not infer an unseen keyword's volume or rank from aggregate traffic. |

Change one metadata field per platform in the next iteration and log the hypothesis. If evidence is insufficient, keep collecting data. |

Do not invent numeric success targets without baseline data. Report increases, decreases, absolute counts, rates with their denominators, and unknowns. A before/after comparison is directional, not a controlled causal test of metadata because the release adds features at the same time.

No paid tools or ads are required. If keyword-level validation remains important later, evaluate access to [Apple Ads keyword popularity and search-term reports](https://ads.apple.com/app-store/help/reporting/0023-reporting-options-and-definitions); popularity is relative, not an exact monthly count, and paid search terms do not prove organic query performance. Prioritize evidence-backed metadata iteration before creative experiments.
