# Price a residential job

Use this guide when the contractor asks for a price, a budget, a bid check or help setting a rate. Start by establishing the job ZIP or metro and country, date, measured quantities and what this contractor supplies. Ask first about the two missing facts most likely to move the total. If all you know is “bathroom remodel,” a historical reference can answer “what have owners spent?” A bid for this bathroom needs the room, selections and trade boundaries. All bundled numbers here use US dollars and US sources. For other countries source the local market and currency directly.

## Choose the evidence for each line

1. Carry forward an agreed contractor figure at its exact coverage. Disclose a material discrepancy with outside prices for contractor review; changing an existing price requires their decision. A budget ceiling, example from a previous chat, wage, retailer minimum or unselected allowance cannot stand in for the contractor's offer.
2. Prefer a dated quote available to this job from a local supplier, subcontractor or installer for the same scope. Record whether tax, kits, old-unit removal, access and minimums are included. Where possible compare two independent observations. If they disagree, check coverage before averaging. One credible exact offer may be shown on its own terms; a starting price establishes only a floor.
3. For a custom job build the estimate from measured labor and materials using the [work package directory](work-packages.md) and [labor and materials guide](inputs.md). Directly supported component prices can be used even when other lines need pricing. Mark an unknown line pending and present a **priced subtotal**. Do not treat an unknown component as zero.
4. For a market comparison use [small service jobs](service.md), [outdoor work](outdoors.md) or [room and house projects](remodels.md). Match work, dimensions, equipment, tier, material responsibility, market and period first. For broad professionally performed US improvements the [historical spending table](historical-project-bands.csv) supplies a separate range check. Bundled installed figures already include some combination of labor and materials; never stack an installed figure and its own components.
5. When the price is material to a decision, refresh source prices for the job date and local store or service area. Save provider URL or quote ID, check date, geography, unit, quantity, any exclusions and the calculation in contractor review. Display observations from another market or year as comparisons with that place and date. Never silently inflate an entire project by CPI, multiply all costs by an area wage ratio, or imply that browsing/verification happened when unavailable.

For Chicago and nearby suburbs, consult the [local installation, service and outdoor cards](chicago.md). Elsewhere, follow the same matching steps with local sources. When using an hourly platform average, verify the contractor’s minimum, billed time and actual crew. A service sold as a fixed package (prehung entry, toilet reset, bundled appliance delivery) needs its own local package check before substituting general labor hours. A retail add-on contingent on a sale, a subcontract bid and a stand-alone service call are different offers. If eligibility changes the result, show a labeled branch or a priced subtotal and ask the deciding question. Unknown room sizes, material grade or return-visit costs are not fixed by multiplying one stand-alone example. Check whether the amount in a completed-job story paid for an extra task absent from this job.

For paint/patch, HVAC, door springs, locks, supplied fixtures and mixed visits use the [component and installed-price checks](completed-job-checks.md). They distinguish production time from the price paid and give dated comparisons with specific inclusions.

## Calculate the job

For each work package show measured quantity × agreed unit price, or crew person-hours × appropriate rates plus delivered materials, applicable subcontract, equipment, disposal and permit charges. Check whether labor rates already recover overhead/profit. Use minimums, deposits, extra trips, markups and taxes only on their specified bases. A margin m gives cost / (1 − m), while a markup u gives cost × (1 + u). Neither is a default. A full build-out can share supervision, transport, dumping and protection across room lines. Exclude the portions the owner contracts directly. Price alternatives separately.

To bound uncertainty, give low/high scenarios with their specific inputs (for example, on-site stock versus a special-order carton and a return visit). Avoid fixed “complex job,” seniority, inflation or luxury multipliers. Decide which checks resolve the spread. Document incompatible or missing evidence and identify precisely what would unlock the next price. Show partial estimates if useful. A range across observed products or markets is not a statistical interval for the job.

## Check the estimate against actual work

When completed-job records are available, keep the original estimate, its date, cost basis, area, scope and quantities. Compare to the final amount for that same scope, with documented owner changes, options, supplier credits and owner-direct purchases reconciled on both sides. Also record the full final invoice for cost-overrun analysis. Group repeat customers, homes or providers together when holding out jobs. Track numeric coverage (including abstentions), mean absolute dollar error, signed bias, fraction within the chosen tolerance, and observed range coverage on held-out jobs. Compare methods on identical targets, and keep posted offers, bids, historical surveys and paid invoices in separate counts. Do not widen ranges or refit factors on the test set and call that an accuracy gain.

## What to deliver

In contractor review distinguish a supplied bid, source-backed planning estimate, historical benchmark, and item awaiting a local quote. Include the source, scope, unit math, place and period, and explain the biggest remaining uncertainty. In homeowner copy show agreed prices as before. When asked for a homeowner budget, label a researched range **Planning estimate — contractor confirmation pending**, state the price date/market and inclusions, and leave unpriced extras outside the total. An optional budget comparison is separate from acceptance of the work. If the contractor only asked to scope a job, keep the existing unpriced workflow.

## Using the historical table

The [2023 national American Housing Survey public-use files, v1.1](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/ahs/2023/) report owner expenditures. Here each row is an unweighted training-sample group of completed owner-occupied projects with most work performed by others and reported positive costs. Use the whole named category and the named region; never assign one of these figures to a labor-only hookup, one unit, square foot or linear foot. Labels that bundle several products cannot separate them. No size, brand, complexity or quality adjustment is identified. Costs are nominal at the time of work in the 2023 survey period. No automatic update to current dollars.

Read p10, median and p90 as a central historical spread, midpoint and sample size, not a contractor offer. For a job with a named metro use its actual local prices first. Use a supported division row (40+ observations) for regional context and show the US row (20+ observations) when the division is missing or when comparing the difference. Division codes: 1 New England, 2 Middle Atlantic, 3 East North Central, 4 West North Central, 5 South Atlantic, 6 East South Central, 7 West South Central, 8 Mountain, 9 Pacific. Use the [Census state-to-division map](https://www2.census.gov/geo/pdfs/maps-data/maps/reference/us_regdiv.pdf) if uncertain.

Example: an East North Central bathroom remodel (type 12) has 105 training observations, median $8,500 and p10–p90 $1,440–$24,600. This is a historical whole-project comparison. For a current walk-in shower with specified tile and glass, build the number from demolition, plumbing, pan and waterproofing, tile, glass, finish, and each party's work.
