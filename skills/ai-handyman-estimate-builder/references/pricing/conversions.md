# Conversions, additions and phased improvements

First decide what the contractor is delivering: a finished room, independent dwelling, newly built footprint, or one trade’s work inside it. Use a published budget only when the stated use, size, included work and market fit. Say whose figure it is, its geography and date, and what is still missing. A local builder’s guide gives planning examples; its high and low numbers are not statistical bounds.

## Project comparisons

| Scope to match | Published comparison in USD | What changes the comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Garage or house space converted into a California ADU | UC Berkeley’s April 2021 owner survey reported median construction costs of $90,000 for the garage group, $100,000 for conversion inside the main residence and $180,000 for new detached ADUs. Garage included horizontal and vertical extensions. Historical statewide owner reports; a detached garage converted in place belongs with conversions. [Survey, Table 2 and footnote](https://www.aducalifornia.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Implementing-the-Backyard-Revolution.pdf) | Confirm whether the unit has a kitchen and bath, utility routes, slab/foundation condition, local design/approval costs and conditioned floor area. For a workshop, price the agreed surfaces, backing and power. For a contemporary ADU budget, obtain current local site, service and build costs; the historical median alone cannot establish a local price. |
| Existing attic converted to living space, Twin Cities | Castle’s 2026 contractor guide, p. 7, gives roughly $66k–$76k job cost for a 375 sq ft finished dry room and $125k–$144k for its 450 sq ft owner-suite example with bath and closet. Both keep the stairs, add railings and extend HVAC. The guide lists design/planning separately; confirm its treatment before stating an all-in total. [Castle guide, February 2026 revision on p. 7](https://www.castlebri.com/cost-guide/2026-twin-cities-remodeling-cost-guide.pdf) | Verify floor capacity, stair and finished headroom, roof changes, bathroom waste route and HVAC capacity. New stairs, dormer or equipment replacement change the scope. For storage decking, price sheets, verified support and access only. |
| Finish a 1,000 sq ft basement, Kansas City metro | Coulston’s 2026 size-specific examples give $35k–$45k for an open recreation room and $45k–$60k with bath and partitioned rooms. Its high-spec example with wet bar/theater runs $60k–$75k or more at that size. Use the size-specific table for this comparison; its general project bands cover a wider mix. [Contractor’s 1,000 sq ft table](https://coulstonconstruction.com/basement-finishing-cost-kansas-city) | Check usable area, moisture, egress where needed, existing rough-in and drain elevation. Excavating for headroom or adding independent dwelling services needs separate design and pricing. A floor replacement in an existing rec room is a flooring package. |
| Build a primary suite on a new foundation, Northeast Ohio | Element’s 2026 custom design-build guide places a one-story 350–650 sq ft bedroom, full bath and closet addition around $200k–$380k including foundation and mechanical trades. Its broader second-story/add-a-story category is $300k–$580k or more and may deliver several rooms. [Element’s Northeast Ohio guide](https://elementkb.com/primary-suite-addition-cost-in-ohio/) | Identify whether footprint or roof changes, which existing surface opens and how it is restored. Price a dry bedroom addition from shell, services and finishes without importing bath allowances. Verify insulation, wet-area finish, glass and closet scope before comparing complete suites. |
| Move laundry upstairs in a two-story Utah house | Cressey’s provider guide lists $8k–$14k for a basement-to-bedroom-level relocation including new services, venting, drain pan and standard cabinets. The $20k–$25k+ example adds a combined mudroom, pet station, lockers and premium surfaces. Undated page, checked November 2026. [Cressey project tiers](https://cresseycustoms.com/services/laundry-relocation) | Confirm dryer type, discharge/vent routes, floor protection, ceiling or wall access and old-room restoration. For an owner-supplied machine placed on existing hookups, use appliance-installation labor and parts. A higher-spec cabinet package cannot establish the price for carrying a washer upstairs. |
| Whole-home work across multiple rooms, Portland metro | Portland Project groups its **signed contracts, 2023–26**, into $150k–$250k for a kitchen, one bath, finishes and limited electrical work; $250k–$450k with structural opening, two baths and partial system updates; $450k–$750k for extensive systems, structural and wet-room work. Excludes land, furnishings and owner-purchased fixtures. Contracts are different from final paid costs. [Contractor’s portfolio summary, updated September 2026](https://www.portlandproject.com/resources/whole-home-remodel-cost-portland/) | For a listing refresh limited to paint and flooring, measure those surfaces. For an occupied, phased renovation, price rooms/visits in sequence, account for moving/protection and temporary service only as agreed, and count the dumpster, HVAC plant, panel and shared plumbing once. No universal phasing surcharge is established here. |

For a dry-room bid inside a larger job, state the shell or trade handoff, the area, the supplied material and the labor price at its actual coverage. For a complete project, itemize owner purchases, contractor work, subcontractors and fees by responsibility. A reported **permit valuation**, if used, must be labeled with the issuing agency, declared scope and date; a building-only valuation may omit electrical/plumbing permits, design and owner purchases. These comparisons use surveys, provider estimates and signed contracts. No new case-level prediction accuracy was measured for these project types.
