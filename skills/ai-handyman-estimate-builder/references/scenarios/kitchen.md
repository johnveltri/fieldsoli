# Kitchen remodel interview

Start with the contractor's piece of the job. “When the old top comes off, which cabinets, finishes and appliances are going back in the same spots? What has the owner hired you to hand back working?” A cabinet installer may finish at template readiness. A remodel lead may own the entire sequence through running water and working appliances. Ask about the interfaces that change that promise. [Parent project](projects.md#kitchen-remodel) · [Scope and scheduling](tasks.md#scope-and-scheduling)

## Gut remodel and changed layout

1. **Mark removal limits.** Ask what comes down to framing, which rooms stay occupied, and what is salvaged, stored or hauled away. If the pantry wall stays, protect it and define the finish line. If it goes, establish who assesses support and service crossings before promising demolition. Where the work disturbs a suspect existing material, identify who releases the area for removal. [Protection and removal](tasks.md#protection-and-removal) · [Structure](tasks.md#foundations-and-structure) · [Material assessment](tasks.md#material-assessment)
2. **Set the layout against real equipment.** Ask for the selected models, sink position, island seating/overhang and door/drawer travel at adjacent corners. Confirmed geometry releases cabinet ordering and counter support details to their owners. An undecided cooktop or panel-ready fridge can hold a specific opening or panel order. Keep it as an owner decision with a due point. [Cabinets and counters](tasks.md#cabinets-and-counters) · [Panel-ready refrigeration](kitchen-water.md#panel-ready-refrigerator-wine-or-beverage-cooler) · [Cooktops](cooking.md#cooktop-or-downdraft-unit)
3. **Trace what moves behind the finishes.** For an island sink, ask where supply, drain and vent will run and whether access below requires opening a finished ceiling. Same-wall reuse may need only agreed reconnections. A move adds plumbing layout and rough-in for the assigned trade and an access/closure decision. For a fuel change, establish who handles the former connection, new supply and final test. A hood on an interior wall needs a confirmed exhaust or recirculation plan before cabinet closure. [Supply](tasks.md#water-supply-and-fixtures) · [Drains](tasks.md#waste-and-drains) · [Fuel](tasks.md#fuel-and-combustion) · [Power](tasks.md#power-and-controls) · [Ventilation](tasks.md#ventilation-and-comfort) · [Exterior penetration](tasks.md#exterior-envelope)
4. **Establish the release before closing walls.** Who places cabinet or hood backing, under-cabinet lighting feeds, receptacles and controls? Who verifies the rough work, obtains any applicable inspections and gives the finish crew the go-ahead? With another trade responsible, name their release as a prerequisite. Where the contractor owns patching, specify the opened wall or ceiling and the paint boundary. A photo of a stain leaves the condition behind it unknown; seek an accessible check or define an agreed stop after exposure. [Backing](tasks.md#mounting-and-backing) · [Lighting and controls](tasks.md#power-and-controls) · [Walls and ceilings](tasks.md#walls-and-ceilings) · [Paint](tasks.md#painting-and-coatings)
5. **Set floor, cabinets and tops in a workable order.** Ask how the specified floor meets fixed cabinets, what height remains at the dishwasher, and who supplies transitions to adjacent rooms. Confirm the order for the chosen flooring system. Agree who levels and secures the bases, fits fillers, confirms overhang support and releases them for the template. If the sink or cooktop is still undecided, identify the affected cutout and owner decision. Write separate timing for floor and backsplash if only one depends on the counters. Leave the floor sequence open until the flooring system is confirmed. After tops are fitted, define the splash endpoints, edge finish and who completes outlets or trim at the new face. [Flooring](tasks.md#flooring) · [Cabinets](tasks.md#cabinets-and-counters) · [Splash and tile edges](tasks.md#tile-and-wet-assemblies) · [Trim](tasks.md#trim-and-millwork)
6. **Return the kitchen to use.** Agree who mounts, connects and tests each appliance and fixture after counters land. Ask which appliances the retailer actually connects, who handles a second visit if cabinetry is late, and what the owner expects working on handoff. Group shared delivery, disposal and testing once. A supplied cabinet labor figure stays on cabinetry across the remodel. Leave other work unpriced until the contractor supplies its pricing.
