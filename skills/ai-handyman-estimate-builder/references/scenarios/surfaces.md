# Surfaces and mounting

## Drywall patch and paint matching

“Where should the paint work end, and do we have the actual color and sheen?” Agreed patch-only touch-up with owner paint → specify who supplies it and the touch-up limit. Corner-to-corner finish → include the named wall and its prep. No usable paint → settle sample/match responsibility before pricing. A color label alone does not establish an invisible blend.

“Is the surface flat, or does the patch have to meet the existing texture?” Flat → include agreed patch, finish, prime and paint. Texture → agree texture repair, sample or stopping point; allow for the confirmed dry-time visits. Overlooked case: a glossy roller patch can flash in side light even with paint from the original can. If appearance matters, agree a test area or wall finish beforehand. If a leak caused the opening, establish who confirms it is resolved before closing. Skip a leak investigation for a disclosed doorknob dent.

## Flooring, isolated planks and transitions

“One interlocking plank by the island: do you have a matching spare in the same locking profile, and from which edge can you reach it?” Verified spare and accessible edge → include the agreed rows lifted and relaid, trim removal/reinstall and one plank. No spare → owner chooses to source compatible stock or approve a different visible result before quoting. Trapped by built-ins → settle feasible access or a product-compatible repair method before committing. Keep quantities approximate until measured.

“For the room replacement, where does the new height meet a door or another floor?” Existing transition confirmed to fit → include reinstall. New height or span requires a different profile → assign selection and installation. Overlooked case: the appliance may no longer clear the counter after a height change; if the run enters that bay, check clearance now and settle extent. Skip asking whole-room square footage for an isolated plank until the repair method actually expands the area.

## Shelves, cabinets and TVs

For mounting billed by a visit minimum, tell the homeowner what the first hour buys. It can cover checking the chosen spots and mounting each piece where support is verified. If a spot lacks backing, leave the affected piece unmounted, show the owner the issue and agree on a different position or a separately approved repair. Keep extra time subject to the contractor’s stated approval terms.

“Where does the requested position put the bracket fasteners relative to the backing you've located?” Confirmed suitable backing and bracket for the intended load → include mounting at that position. Mismatch → owner chooses an acceptable shifted position or contractor scopes backing access, patch and paint before quoting. If suitability remains unknown, leave mounting readiness open.

“What does finished mean for the leads or cabinet doors at that location?” Surface cord cover agreed → include that cover and endpoint. Owner wants a relocated receptacle → define specialist handoff and confirm who arranges it. For cabinets, check door swing against adjacent trim before fixing the end position. Overlooked case: an articulating TV can bind a short cable at full extension. If the arm moves, confirm cable reach and include a movement check or assign the replacement lead. Skip interviewing about streaming subscriptions for a physical mount.
