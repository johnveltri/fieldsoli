# Patios and walkways

“Show me where the pavers rock or dip, where water goes after rain, and which areas they want changed.” Start with the promised result: a defined repair, relaying existing paving, or a replacement patio with a new path. Confirm the surface assembly before choosing the repair: loose-laid concrete pavers, stone on bedding, mortared stone and poured concrete need different work.

| Part of the job | Decision-changing question or check | Estimate effect and smaller tasks |
|---|---|---|
| Limits and measurements | Mark the repair boundary or new outline. Is a walkway part of this job? Measure patio area and path length/width; does the path measurement start outside the patio? | Keep repair area separate from total patio area. For replacement, count shared intersections once and record width, turns, landings and tie-ins. An owner's approximate measurement remains approximate. [Scope and scheduling](tasks.md#scope-and-scheduling) |
| Uneven paver diagnosis | Are units intact but rocking, joints washed out, edges spreading, or whole areas settling? Check edge restraint and runoff; agree on a small lift-and-inspect area if the cause is concealed. | Stable support with localized bedding/edge trouble → lift and reuse the agreed units, correct the affected bedding/edge and reinstate joints. Failed base, roots or persistent water → agree on the cause repair before promising a lasting surface reset. Do not treat extra bedding as a substitute for a failed base. [Masonry and paving](tasks.md#masonry-and-paving) · [Groundwork](tasks.md#groundwork) · [Drainage](tasks.md#drainage-and-gutters) |
| Salvage and matching | Which units are reusable, cracked or unavailable? Are owner spares the right size, thickness and color? What happens if units break during lifting? | Record reuse versus new supply, agreed replacements and visible mismatch. Clarify ordinary handling breakage coverage rather than automatically making it extra. A repair within one corner does not include washing or sealing the whole patio. [Masonry and paving](tasks.md#masonry-and-paving) · [Cleaning](tasks.md#cleaning-and-seasonal-service) |
| Access, protection and removal | Can the equipment and loaded barrows reach the work through the gate? Who moves furniture? Which paving, old base, lawn and roots must come out, and where does each go? | Assign carry route, protection, demolition, salvage storage, sod stripping and loading/haul-away. Agree one disposal scope or distinct non-overlapping charges. Do not infer free salvage, reuse credits or owner disposal. [Protection and removal](tasks.md#protection-and-removal) · [Haul away](tasks.md#moving-and-haul-away) · [Fence access](fences-gates.md) |
| Excavation and base | Who confirms utility responses and private irrigation/lighting routes before digging? What assembly and compacted base depth has the installer selected for this soil, use and paving product? | Define excavation, subgrade preparation, base material, compaction and bedding as separate operations within the agreed rate or bundle. Assign unsuitable-soil or obstruction decisions only where unresolved. Do not prescribe one base depth for every site or confuse compacted volume with delivered loose material. [Groundwork](tasks.md#groundwork) · [Yard and irrigation](tasks.md#yard-and-irrigation) |
| Drainage and tie-ins | Check finished elevations at the house, door, steps, gate and existing walk. Where will runoff discharge? Is the owner asking for a new drain or just restoring the agreed surface fall? | Carry the agreed drainage direction into the work and check transitions without promising a flat surface. If a drain or outlet is needed, identify its route, discharge and responsible installer before closing paving. Keep flashing, thresholds and gate clearance intact; resolve conflicts before quoting final grades. [Drainage](tasks.md#drainage-and-gutters) · [Exterior envelope](tasks.md#exterior-envelope) · [Fences and gates](tasks.md#fences-and-gates) |
| Paving, borders and joints | Which product, thickness, pattern and border? What edge restraint and jointing system goes with it? Are curves or cuts included? | For conventional pavers, assign supply, laying, cuts, restraints, joints and specified final compaction. Permeable paving needs its own aggregate, joint and drainage system; do not substitute conventional sand automatically. For stone, large slabs, mortared surfaces or concrete, establish the appropriate base/bonding or formwork, reinforcement, joints, finish and curing scope with the installer. [Masonry and paving](tasks.md#masonry-and-paving) |
| New walkway and adjacent work | Does the path cross lawn, a sprinkler line or a grade break? Must a mower, wheelchair or other equipment pass through? Are lighting, steps or rails actually requested? | Add the net new path area, sod removal, tie-ins and agreed clear width. Schedule necessary sleeves and service relocations before paving. Add steps, rails, power or accessible-route design only when the route and use call for them. | Restoration and handoff | Raked soil, seed or sod along the edges? Who replaces furniture and handles watering or a later sealer visit? How will drainage and transitions be checked? | State restored areas and finish limits, clean up and remove agreed debris, check rocking units, joint completion, transitions, gate operation and agreed runoff direction. Give product-specific return-to-use guidance. Treat sealer as a selection, not an automatic inclusion. [Landscaping](lawn-landscaping.md) · [Coatings](tasks.md#painting-and-coatings) · [Handoff](tasks.md#handoff) |

Ask two to four connected questions at a time. Skip digging and design questions for a confirmed surface-only reset unless inspection reveals a reason. For a replacement, resolve layout, grades, access and selected assembly before treating the price as ready to send; do not keep investigating a repair the owner declined.

Preserve the contractor's supplied rate coverage. Installed square footage, purchased paver quantity with cuts/waste, cubic yards of base and loads of spoil are different quantities. Do not add material waste to billable installed area unless the contractor explicitly prices that way. A new path is not another demolition area if it starts as lawn.

Where lifting exposes an unresolved failure, name the trigger (for example, soft base beneath the marked repair), the consequence (repair cannot proceed on that support), and the action: show the homeowner, quote the revised work and receive authorization before going beyond the agreed scope. Do not make normal work already included in the price conditional.

Technical basis: [CMHA's maintenance guide](https://www.cmha.org/resource/pav-tec-006/) distinguishes surface distress, edge restraint, base repair and reinstatement. Its [construction guide](https://www.cmha.org/resource/pav-tec-009/) warns against correcting a deficient base with extra bedding sand. These inform scoping questions, not a site-specific construction design or a price list.
