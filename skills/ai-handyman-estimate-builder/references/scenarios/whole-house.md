# Whole-house renovation interview

“When you hand back the keys, which rooms will look different, which systems will work differently, and what part have you agreed to deliver?” Start with the room or phase the contractor knows best. Ask whether the family stays, who coordinates the trades, and what the site will look like on the contractor’s first visit. A kitchen can be stripped to studs while the bedrooms receive paint only.

For each affected room, keep a short working record of what stays, what comes out, what goes back, the handoff owner and any quoted quantity or price. For a shared item, record its extent once and refer to it from the rooms served. A floor price across three rooms stays together. A single dumpster, plumbing stack or electrical shutdown can serve several rooms. If responsibility or cost coverage is unclear, ask before assigning it. ## Full gut rehab

1. **Set the demolition boundary and protect the building.** Room by room, mark finishes, partitions and systems to remove, salvage or keep. “Down to studs” leaves decisions about subfloors, exterior sheathing, windows and existing services. Who isolates utilities, establishes structural support and weather protection, assesses disturbed materials, and arranges hauling? Separate known removals from concealed discoveries. If exposure shows a specific failure outside the agreed repair, show it, settle the repair owner and get approval for any additional price before proceeding. [Demolition and protection](tasks.md#protection-and-removal) · [Material assessment](tasks.md#material-assessment) · [Structure](tasks.md#foundations-and-structure) · [Envelope](tasks.md#exterior-envelope) · [Addition at the existing house](room-addition.md)
2. **Make the shell ready for rough work.** Who verifies bearing walls, altered openings, support from roof to foundation, and a dry enclosure? Confirm what the GC or structural crew hands over and who repairs disturbed exterior finishes. Extend the interview only where an actual project affects the shell. [Windows](tasks.md#windows-and-screens) · [Water intrusion](tasks.md#water-intrusion-and-recovery) · [Basement build-out](basement.md) · [Attic conversion](attic.md) · [Garage conversion](garage-conversion.md) · [Deck or porch junction](deck-porch.md)
3. **Trace shared services before closing.** Follow moved fixtures through the floor below and appliances back to their supplies. Does the upstairs bath drain cross the kitchen ceiling? Does the laundry move affect the same plumbing stack or exhaust penetration? Assign design, rough work, testing and access restoration by location. Confirm who owns power capacity, heating/air distribution, bath and kitchen exhaust, and alarms. A route that still needs verification stays open; keep the affected bay accessible until settled. 4. **Agree who releases each area for insulation and drywall.** Which rooms can close independently? Confirm backing for cabinets, glass and bars, responsibility for air sealing and insulation, and applicable permit/inspection releases before covering work. For a finish-only crew, distinguish receiving bare studs from receiving hung, finished or primed walls. Price the actual handoff. 5. **Sequence the room finishes against each other.** Who needs finished elevations before setting cabinets, transitions or doors? Establish kitchen cabinet release for counter templating, bath waterproofing/test before tile, glass measurement after tile, and floors ready for appliance loads. State who paints trim and where one flooring crew hands off to another. Keep wet-area tile separate from any dry-room plank quantity. 6. **Return working systems and rooms to the homeowner.** Who makes final connections, tests appliances, checks fixtures and ventilation, and repairs openings left for those trades? Agree whether the contractor hands over a completed finish package, a working room or the whole home. Confirm walkthrough, keys, deferred deliveries, return visits and any final sign-off owner. Closing one room does not establish that the family can move back into the house.
