# Research basis and coverage

The index uses U.S. homeowner language and service-market examples. Directory categories establish the kinds of requests people encounter. Individual listings illustrate actual combinations of work. Neither establishes national booking volume, licensing eligibility or typical price. Project relationships and interview questions are editorial synthesis. Check the chosen product manual and local requirements when a real quote turns on them.

Deck scoping draws on the American Wood Council’s residential wood deck guide for the relationships among footings, beams, posts, ledger, guards and stairs. Its stated scope is limited. The porch branch asks for the project-specific roof support detail. Trex’s resurfacing guidance informs the compatibility and border-blocking questions; use the selected product and local project requirements for actual dimensions. [AWC deck guide](https://awc.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/AWC-DCA62015-DeckGuide-1804.pdf) · [AWC on canopies](https://awc.org/faq/does-the-dca6-apply-to-canopies-or-awnings/) · [Trex resurfacing guidance](https://www.trex.com/academy/how-to-guides/all-guides/how-to-resurface-a-wood-deck-with-trex-transcend-composite-decking/)

The garage conversion interview uses Portland’s garage-to-ADU pathway as a local example of checking intended use, shell suitability, site plans, utilities and trade responsibilities. Actual approvals and service requirements follow the project address. [Portland ADU conversion guidance](https://www.portland.gov/ppd/residential-permitting/home-projects/accessory-dwelling-units)

The attic playbook uses Portland’s conversion guidance as a local example of how floor capacity, finished headroom, stairs and escape openings affect residential use. DOE guidance informs the questions about ventilation, insulation continuity and service access in a storage attic. Confirm the design and jurisdiction for the actual address.
