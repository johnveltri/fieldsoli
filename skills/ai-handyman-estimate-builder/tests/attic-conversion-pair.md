# Paired conversation test for an attic conversion

Two evaluators independently read the skill and attic playbook. Each received the same intake and gave an interview turn before receiving a contractor reply.

“The Morgans want the attic usable. About 200 sq ft of sheet decking, $1,450 labor to lay the sheets only. Help me scope it.”

Both asked about intended use, the boundaries of the sheeted area, support for the intended load, and the route for getting sheets upstairs. Neither priced an entire room from the area or assumed the deck was ready to carry stored goods.

## A. Seasonal storage

**Contractor answer** — Seasonal bins in the marked area, about 200 sq ft. Engineer verified the framing for that use and specified raised sleepers. Contractor supplies and builds sleepers, lays owner-supplied sheets and leaves removable access over a junction box. Insulation depth and eave ventilation paths checked. Sheets cut to fit the measured hatch, bins fit, existing ladder stays. Only labor to lay sheets priced.

## B. Bedroom and full bath

**Contractor answer** — Approved layout addresses finished headroom, permanent stair and sleeping-room escape opening. Structural crew reinforces the floor and builds stairs and rails. Window crew installs and flashes the gable window. Contractor lays about 200 sq ft of owner-supplied structural subfloor in the bedroom only for $1,450 labor. Contractor frames knee walls, insulates per plan, drywalls, paints and trims both rooms outside the shower. Separate flooring crew installs the bedroom finish floor. Bath crew owns bath substrate, shower waterproofing, test and all tile. Plumber owns supply, waste, vent and fixture installation/testing. Stack route unverified. Opening and restoring the occupied ceiling below are unassigned. Mechanical crew owns heat and bath exhaust; electrician owns wiring and alarms. Affected framing and concealed services must be released before covering. Other contractor work unpriced.

## Observed homeowner drafts and acceptance check

| Check | Storage | Bedroom and bathroom |
|---|---|---|
| What the price buys | “Labor to lay the decking sheets — $1,450.” About 200 sq ft of raised platform for seasonal bins. | “Bedroom structural subfloor — $1,450 labor.” About 200 sq ft in the bedroom. Separate flooring crew installs the finish floor and separate bath crew owns the bathroom substrate and tile. |
| Sequence and responsibility | Contractor supplies and installs specified sleepers, then fits the owner’s sheets with a removable section. Existing hatch and ladder serve the platform. | Structural crew reinforces and builds stairs/rails. Window crew fits and flashes the escape window. Contractor covers released framing and services, frames knee walls, insulates, drywalls, paints and trims outside the shower. |
| Open items | Sleeper supply and installation unpriced. Contractor review asked whether fitting the removable section falls within sheet-laying labor. | Plumber verifies the bathroom drain route. The draft leaves opening and restoring the occupied ceiling below pending agreement. Framing and finishes stay unpriced. |
| Irrelevant branches dropped | No residential stairs, escape window, bath or HVAC package. | No seasonal storage platform, hatch enlargement or blanket ceiling repair charge. |

Both drafts used “Scope draft — pricing to follow.” The storage test uncovered a detail to retain in contractor review: an agreed access panel can be part of the work even while its coverage under a narrow installation price is unresolved. To repeat the test, provide only the opening message, reveal one answer, and check the resulting homeowner scope against the corresponding column. The two complete paths were exercised; individual alternatives such as a dormer addition were not separately tested.
