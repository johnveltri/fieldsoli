# Same yard, two estimates

Two independent evaluators received the same contractor intake and used the lawn and landscaping playbook. To replay, give the intake first, then supply one of the contractor replies and request homeowner copy.

“Lopez yard, about 3,000 sq ft of front and back lawn, 36-inch side gate. Two rectangles marked in the back lawn, each measured 6 by 12 ft. They mentioned edging. My lawn service is $65 per visit. Help scope it.”

The rectangles are still turf in both scenarios. Marking them does not authorize a cut. Test whether the first questions establish what “edging” means, what gets through the gate, and which work the quoted rate covers.

Both first turns asked whether to maintain existing edges or cut new outlines, what the $65 covers and whether the mower clears the gate. One also asked what should be inside the marked areas at handoff.

## A. Weekly visits

The contractor confirmed weekly maintenance only, including a routine first cut. Mow all turf, preserve the proposed bed markings, string-trim the fence, edge the sidewalk and driveway, mulch clippings into the turf, blow paths clean and latch the gate. The mower fits. The contractor supplied $65 per completed visit and no fixed season length.

The homeowner draft quoted **$65 per completed visit** for weekly front and back mowing across about 3,000 sq ft. It explicitly mowed inside both rectangles and preserved the markings. It included the named trimming, edging, clippings, cleanup and gate duties. It added neither a first-cut surcharge nor an assumed number of visits.

## B. One-time installation

The contractor confirmed the two marked outlines for installation. Remove and haul the sod, prepare both beds, supply and install six one-gallon perennials per bed from the approved list, apply shredded hardwood mulch 3 inches deep, water in once and sweep. The owner waters afterward. The contractor verified the wheelbarrow fits, the truck can stage in the drive and the sprinkler layout has been checked. The contractor supplied a single $980 fixed price covering labor, materials and disposal. No mowing booked.

The homeowner draft quoted **$980 for both beds, including labor, materials and sod disposal**. It explicitly included cutting the two approved outlines, lifting, loading and removing the sod from the property, planting twelve perennials, mulching, initial watering and cleanup. The owner took over watering after installation. The $65 service rate did not appear in the homeowner estimate.

## Results

- Same site, different boundaries. The first estimate maintained the hardscape edges and all existing turf. The second converted 144 sq ft of that turf into beds.
- Same gate, different access check. The mowing answer confirmed the mower fits. The install answer confirmed wheelbarrow access from the driveway.
- Quantities matched the inputs. Two beds × 6 ft × 12 ft = 144 sq ft. At the specified depth, 144 sq ft × 0.25 ft = 36 cu ft (1⅓ cu yd) nominal mulch coverage. The calculation appeared in contractor review only and did not create a separate purchase quantity or charge.
- Both runs passed the scope, quantity, waste-handling and price-coverage checks. Neither draft introduced a conditional extra. Both prices were supplied by the test contractor solely for these cases.
