# Conversation tests

Start each exchange independently. Give the assistant only the intake. Reveal the reply after the question; accept equivalent questions that change the same decision. Compare the resulting estimate and contractor review with the expected edits. Prices in these fixtures apply only to the individual case.

## 1. Dictated walkthrough and correction

Contractor: “Um, back bedroom door drags, customer wants it swapped, there's a new door leaned in the hall.”
Probe: “Are you hanging a new slab in the existing jamb, and is that door already machined for the hinges and latch? That determines what fitting to include.”
Reply: “Actually they want to keep the door. That spare belongs upstairs. I traced the drag to the top hinge screws; wood behind them is sound. I'll secure it, adjust and test the latch. No cutting.”
Expected: replace the swap with securing and adjusting one existing door, then checking swing and latch. Retire machining, painting cut edges and spare-door responsibility. A generic answer assumes a replacement. Asking about the spare's species after the correction is excessive.

## 2. Detailed existing estimate

Contractor: “Review this wording: ‘Hang two owner-supplied bedroom slabs, transfer hinges and knobs, adjust to latch. $480 labor for both. Owner paints.’ Slabs are measured and machined.”
Probe: “Does ‘owner paints’ include any newly fitted edges, and when do they want the doors handed back for paint?”
Reply: “Yes, after hanging. Add ‘Owner paints after fitting, including any trimmed edges.’ Keep the rest.”
Expected: show that proposed wording before rewriting; preserve every other commitment and the $480 group price. A generic rewrite can turn the lump sum into two invented rates. Asking again for measurements or knob finishes adds noise.

## 3. Two repairs, shared visit

Contractor: “Trip to hang two laundry shelves and patch a doorknob hole. Studs checked. I supply the brackets. They want the wall painted too.”
Probe: “Are the shelves on the wall being patched, and where does the paint stop? That determines the sequence and return visit.”
Reply: “Different rooms. Shelves go up first visit. Patch and prime in hall, return to paint just the patch once dry. Owner supplies paint and shelves; I supply other materials.”
Expected: two mounted shelves; one patched, primed and touched-up area; agreed return for paint, owner materials assigned, no blanket trip fee. Generic assistant may promise a whole-wall repaint. Asking wall color after owner has matching paint in hand would add no scope.

## 4. Owner purchase blocks scheduling

Contractor: “Install owner’s new vanity and faucet, labor only. Want the scope now. The display cabinet had doors, the one delivered has drawers.”
Probe: “Can you line up the actual drawer travel against the trap and stops before quoting?”
Reply: “Drawer hits the trap. Owner will exchange for the door version and confirm clearance before I schedule. Top and faucet fit. Same footprint, owner takes the old one; I'll disconnect, swap, reconnect and test.”
Expected: keep owner exchange and fit check as pre-scheduling responsibility; base describes the specified in-place swap and test. Generic assistant may charge for plumbing relocation. Asking about moving the drain after exchange is settled is excessive.

## 5. Photo with concealed surface

Contractor: “Photo of peeling paint along the bottom of this outside window trim. They want me to fix the trim; can you draft now?”
Probe: “Where can you check for firm wood now, and which faces will stay hidden until a board comes off?”
Reply: “Visible face is firm; haven't checked the accessible lower end. I'll probe it tomorrow before deciding paint prep versus replacement.”
Expected: provisional draft describes requested finish, flags the end check and pending method before sending. No confirmed decay, invented footage or automatic rot surcharge. Generic assistant diagnoses from the image. Asking for framing-replacement dimensions before the accessible inspection is excessive.

## 6. Several legitimate discovery outcomes

Contractor: “Replace about 8 linear feet of painted exterior casing, seal and touch up with my paint. Adjacent casing and framing covered until removal. Trim repair only.”
Probe: “Where does your trim repair end if the next piece is soft, and what happens if the softness continues into the framing?”
Reply: “Extra casing $18 per linear foot, I'll show and measure it and get text approval on the amount. If framing is soft I stop and have the owner arrange a carpenter's assessment. I only resume after they resolve it and we agree on scope.”
Expected: approximate 8 ft base with finish, unit rate only for measured additional casing, separate framing stop and handoff. No price for framing, no estimated extension and no implied acceptance. Generic assistant collapses both triggers into hidden damage. Asking how much structural repair the handyman will perform ignores the stated boundary.

## 7. Simple job, dismiss a rare risk

Contractor: “Swap six pantry pulls, same centers, screws tested, same finish, no drilling. $90 for all six. Homeowner has them and wants the old pulls back.”
Probe: “Do the new feet cover the old marks, or did they ask you to touch up the doors? That sets the finish handoff.”
Reply: “Checked on a door. They cover. Just swap and hand back the old ones.”
Expected: six supplied pulls installed in existing holes, old pulls returned, $90 total, zero conditions. Generic assistant invents stripped-screw or refinishing charges. Investigating concealed utilities for a no-drilling swap is over-questioning.

## 8. Interlocking floor and immediate draft

Contractor: “Draft now. One busted click plank by a closet. Same-run spare box. About three rows from the wall, I think. $240 includes pulling and replacing shoe. Picture shows a dark line along the joint.”
Probe: “Can those rows lift from the shoe side without the closet jamb trapping them? That determines whether the repair fits your quoted method.”
Reply: “Yes, jamb is clear. Three rows exactly. Dark line is pencil. I supply the shoe nails. Owner sets out box.”
Expected: first draft retains the $240 bundle and approximate access extent, asks about access; final scope reflects confirmed rows, shoe reinstatement and owner supply. No damage charge. Generic assistant asks for square footage. Investigating moisture after the pencil correction adds noise.
