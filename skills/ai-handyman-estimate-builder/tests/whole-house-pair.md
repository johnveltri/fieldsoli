# Whole-house renovation paired conversation test

Two independent evaluators read the skill and whole-house interview. Each received the same intake, answered before seeing its contractor reply, and then drafted the estimate. To repeat the test, reveal only one branch per conversation.

“I’m bidding the Chen whole-house renovation. About 1,100 sq ft of dry-room plank. $5,200 labor to lay that plank only. Help me scope it.”

Both asked what changes in each room, where the plank ends, who owns the work, and whether the Chens will stay during construction. One also asked about material supply and subfloor readiness.

## Occupied cosmetic refresh

**Contractor reply** — Owners move furniture room by room and need a path through the hall to their only bathroom. Plank in bedrooms, hall and living room ends at existing kitchen and bathroom tile. Contractor protects, removes and hauls carpet, refits base and two transitions, patches nail holes and paints dry-room walls to the corners. Ceilings excluded. Contractor swaps bathroom vanity pulls in existing holes. Kitchen untouched. Owner supplies plank, paint and pulls. Subfloor checked and suitable. Only laying plank is priced.

## Vacant gut rehab

**Contractor reply** — Contractor handles one whole-house protection, demolition and haul-away scope down to studs, insulation, drywall/paint after release, dry-room plank, base, doors and kitchen cabinets. GC owns the bearing opening, dry shell and final inspections. Plumber owns repipe, stacks, fixtures and testing. Upstairs bath drain route through kitchen ceiling still undecided. Electrician handles rewiring and alarms, mechanical contractor handles HVAC/exhaust. Bath crew owns pan, waterproofing/test and tile. Stone crew templates after cabinets, installs the top and mounts the sink. Retailer sets machines. Plumber final-connects the sink and dishwasher. Owner supplies plank. Same flooring areas and price coverage. All other contractor work unpriced.

## Observed homeowner estimates

| Check | Cosmetic refresh | Gut rehab |
|---|---|---|
| Access and sequence | Owner moves furniture room by room. Contractor review asks the family to confirm the hallway sequence so their only bathroom stays accessible. | Vacant house. One shared protection, demolition and haul-away scope. GC provides bearing opening and dry shell. |
| Walls and services | Patch nail holes and paint bedroom, hall and living-room walls corner to corner with owner paint. | Insulate, hang and finish drywall and paint as each area is released. Keep the kitchen ceiling bay open until the upstairs bath drain route is settled. |
| Kitchen and bath | Two transitions to retained tile and a swap of owner-supplied vanity pulls in existing holes. | Cabinets release the stone template; stone crew installs top and mounts sink. Retailer sets appliances and plumber connects sink/dishwasher. Bath crew owns pan, waterproofing, test and tile. |
| Responsibility | Contractor scopes finishes and removal. The draft adds no system upgrade or demolition of untouched rooms. | Plumber, electrician and mechanical contractor own their stated trades; GC handles final inspections. No duplicate whole-house demolition under room packages. |
| Pricing | $5,200 covers only laying about 1,100 sq ft of owner-supplied plank in the named dry rooms. Other agreed work pending price. | Same price coverage and approximate area; bathroom tile and trade costs stay outside the dry-room figure. |

The refresh review flagged a detail still needed before work starts: agree on a feasible hall sequence with the family. The gut draft explicitly held the affected ceiling bay open without delaying areas already released. Both used “Scope draft — pricing to follow.” These tests exercised the two complete scenarios; a mixed refresh/gut sequence in an occupied house was not tested separately.
