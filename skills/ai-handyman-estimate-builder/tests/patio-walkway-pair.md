# Same patio, two estimates

Two independent evaluators used the [patio and walkway playbook](../references/scenarios/patios-walkways.md) and the skill entrypoint. Give this intake first, then supply either reply below:

“The Bell yard has a 12 by 16 paver patio. The pavers rock in one 4 by 6 corner, and the owner marked a 3 by 20 strip of lawn from the patio toward the gate. Help me estimate it.”

Both evaluators asked whether to repair the corner or redo the patio, whether the marked strip was approved for installation now, and whether it started outside the patio. Both asked about the condition of units, edge, bedding/base and runoff before pricing the repair. Neither priced a path from markings alone.

## A. Reset the uneven corner

Contractor reply: “Repair only; the walk is for later. I lifted the rocking units to check: intact loose-laid pavers, firm base with no washout; the bedding is uneven and 6 ft of edge restraint is loose. I'll lift and reuse all the pavers in that 4 × 6 area, refasten the 6 ft of edge, correct the bedding, reset to the adjoining pitch away from the house, compact and refill the joints. Check for rocking and a smooth transition, and sweep up. $480 fixed, including all labor and materials. No new pavers, sealer or lawn work. Draft it now.”

Observed customer draft:

> **Bell patio corner repair**
>
> I’ll lift and reuse the existing pavers in the 4-by-6-foot corner (24 square feet), refasten 6 feet of edge restraint, and correct the uneven bedding. I’ll reset the pavers to match the adjoining patio’s slope away from the house, compact them, and refill the joints.
>
> I’ll check that the repaired pavers sit firmly without rocking, verify a smooth transition to the adjoining patio, and sweep up. The work is limited to the marked corner and its edge, with no new pavers, sealer, or lawn work.
>
> **Fixed total, including all labor and materials — $480.**

## B. Replace the patio and add the walkway

Contractor reply: “Replace the whole patio and build the walkway now. Measured patio 12 × 16 ft, walk 3 × 20 ft starting outside the patio edge to the gate. Owner approved standard gray concrete pavers in running bond on both. $24 per installed sq ft includes materials, labor, excavation, new compacted aggregate base, bedding, cuts, edge restraints, compaction and joint sand. $900 flat covers removing old patio pavers and base, stripping the walkway sod, loading and hauling all removed material and excavation spoil. $300 flat for topsoil and seed along disturbed margins of both areas; owner waters. Door and gate clearance, equipment route and fall away from the house to the lawn checked; no new drain. I'll arrange utility marking and verify private lines before digging. Check transitions, rocking, gate swing and surface fall at finish. Draft it now.”

Observed customer draft:

> **Bell patio replacement and new walkway**
>
> I’ll replace the 12-by-16-ft patio and build a 3-by-20-ft walkway from the patio edge to the gate. Both surfaces will use your approved standard gray concrete pavers in a running-bond pattern.

| Observed item | Price |
|---|---:|
| Remove the old patio pavers and base, strip the sod along the walkway, and load and haul all removed material and excavation spoil. | $900 |
| Install 252 sq ft of paving at $24 per installed sq ft. Includes materials, labor, excavation, new compacted aggregate base, bedding, paver cuts, edge restraints, final compaction and joint sand. | $6,048 |
| Place topsoil and seed along the disturbed margins of the patio and walkway. You’ll water the seeded areas. | $300 |
| **Total** | **$7,248** |

The draft also assigned utility marking and private-line verification to the contractor, noted door/gate clearance and equipment access, specified slope away from the house to the lawn, and included checks for rocking, transitions, gate swing and finished fall.

## Acceptance checks

- **Repair:** 4 × 6 = 24 sq ft within the existing patio. Quote the supplied lump sum once, reuse intact units, repair exactly 6 feet of restraint and leave the marked lawn alone. Passed.
- **Replacement:** 12 × 16 + 3 × 20 = 252 installed sq ft. The 24 sq ft corner is already in the patio; no repair add-on. Only the patio has old paving to remove. Passed.
- **Rate coverage:** 252 × $24 = $6,048; add one $900 removal/haul charge and one $300 restoration charge. No waste markup, second disposal fee, drain, or invented rate. Passed.
- **Handoff:** Repaired corner follows existing pitch and meets retained paving; new work also reaches the gate, which must clear. Seeded margins and owner watering appear only on the replacement. Passed.

Difference: $7,248 − $480 = $6,768. All dollar figures are fictional contractor inputs for testing, not recommended rates.

## Correction test: soft base discovered

Follow the repair run with: “Hold on, the base is firm under most of the 24 sq ft but I found a soft pocket when I lifted the last row. Can't tell how far it goes yet. The $480 is for the surface reset only and doesn't cover rebuilding base. Homeowner has not approved more work or any new price. Give me a corrected draft now.”

Observed: the evaluator removed the blanket assurance that the entire base was firm. It kept $480 attached only to the surface reset, identified the last row as the point where work pauses, and said the contractor would show the owner the pocket. It required approval for any added investigation cost, then a scope, price and separate authorization before rebuilding the base and resetting that row. No speculative allowance or revised total. Passed.

Scope of testing: these runs exercise conventional loose-laid pavers, one measured rectangular path and contractor-supplied rates. They do not establish a base design, local price or performance for other paving systems.
