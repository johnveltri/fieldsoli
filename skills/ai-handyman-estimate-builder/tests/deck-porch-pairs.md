# Deck remodel paired conversation test

Two evaluators independently read the skill and deck/porch playbook. Both received this opening message before seeing a contractor answer: “The Nguyens want the back deck redone, about 240 sq ft. $2,200 labor to lay the deck field. Help me scope it.”

Both asked what comes off, what carries the new boards, who supplies materials, and who handles removal. One asked whether new boards clear the retained rail posts, door and top step. The other asked how the family would enter the house during work. Replay each branch separately; score estimate decisions, quantities, responsibilities and price coverage.

## A. Resurfacing

**Contractor reply:** Open deck. Existing frame and connections checked, sound and compatible with straight composite layout. Contractor removes and hauls field boards. Owner supplies boards and fasteners. Posts and rails stay untouched. No border. Fascia and stairs stay. Thickness matches at door and top step. Other entrance available. Only field labor priced.

**Observed homeowner output:** “I’ll remove and haul away the existing deck field boards. Pricing for this work is pending.” The installation line carried $2,200 for about 240 square feet of straight composite on the existing frame with owner-supplied boards and fasteners. The estimate retained rails, posts, fascia and stairs and identified the alternate entrance. The review asked for removal/haul-away pricing. No footing allowance, structural condition or rail replacement was added.

## B. Rebuilding

**Contractor reply:** Tear out the old attached deck and rebuild freestanding on the same footprint to the approved plan. Contractor demolishes and hauls, excavates and pours six new footings after required excavation inspection, frames posts/beams/joists and lays straight composite. Contractor builds one new stair flight, deck guards, stair guards and stair handrail. Owner supplies field boards and fasteners. Owner's siding contractor closes the old ledger location. Side entrance available. Owner's mason supplies landing, final elevation unconfirmed. Same field labor price; other work unpriced.

**Observed homeowner output:** “I’ll lay out and excavate six new footings, pour them after the required excavation inspection, and install the posts, beams and joists.” The estimate carried $2,200 only for laying about 240 sq ft, identified the siding contractor's closure after demolition, and included one stair flight, deck and stair guards and the handrail. “Your mason will provide the landing. I’ll confirm its finished elevation with the mason before cutting the stair stringers.” The draft flagged incomplete pricing. Contractor review also asked whether old footings come out and who handles excavation spoil.

## Compare the estimates

| Check | Resurface | Rebuild |
|---|---|---|
| Removal | Existing field boards | Old attached deck; old-footing disposal still open |
| Supporting structure | Confirmed existing frame stays | Six new footings and new posts, beams and joists |
| House interface | Matching height at retained door and top step | Siding contractor closes former ledger location |
| Stairs and guards | Retained | New flight, deck guards, stair guards and handrail. Mason's landing elevation needed first |
| Price and quantities | $2,200 field installation labor, about 240 sq ft | Same price coverage and approximate area. No invented footing sizes, riser counts, rail footage or total |

## Supplemental covered-porch run

Intake: porch floor replacement beneath a retained roof, round columns and removable screens. The evaluator asked where the columns bear. The contractor confirmed plans show the floor removal would disturb column bearing, assigned support design/work and reinstatement to the owner’s engineer and porch specialist, and limited the flooring contractor to released sections and screen handling.

The resulting draft kept structural work with the assigned specialists, waited for their go-ahead per section, included screen labeling/storage/refitting, named the side entrance and stayed unpriced. The evaluator flagged screen refitting after column reinstatement as an unresolved return-visit question. The final guide now asks who releases the restored openings and whether a return visit is needed.
