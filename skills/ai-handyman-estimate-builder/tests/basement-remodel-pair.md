# Paired conversation test · Lee basement

Two independent evaluators used the estimate skill and basement playbook. Both received the same first message. Each produced an interview turn before receiving its contractor reply. To repeat, reveal one branch per conversation and check scope, timing, price coverage and unresolved items in the actual output.

**Intake:** “The Lees want their basement redone. Roughly 450 sq ft of plank. My $1,900 is for laying plank only. Help me scope the job.”

Both interviewers asked about existing conditions, intended use, rooms and transitions, and whether the substrate could be checked. Neither treated “redone” as permission to quote a bedroom conversion on the first turn.

## A. Existing finished family room

**Contractor reply:** Same family-room use. Remove and haul carpet and pad, reinstall base, patch nail holes and paint all four walls. Existing ceiling, stairs and utility room stay. Plank stops at bottom landing. Utility-tile transition fits. Slab checked, dry and flat for selected plank. Owner moves furniture and supplies plank. Price is laying labor only. Draft now.

**Observed estimate:** Owner moves furniture and supplies plank. Contractor removes and hauls carpet and pad, lays roughly 450 sq ft to the agreed boundaries, reinstalls baseboards, patches nail holes and paints four walls. The draft identifies $1,900 for laying plank only and leaves removal, base and painting work unpriced. Contractor review asked who supplies paint. No drainage investigation, egress work, bedroom or bath package was added.

## B. Build-out from bare shell

**Contractor reply:** New bedroom, rec room and full bath. Owner supplies plank for bedroom and rec room only. Local plan and escape-window detail approved. Owner’s crew cuts the opening, fits and drains the well; contractor trims inside. Slab checked and suitable. Contractor frames partitions, insulates to plan, drywalls, paints and hangs doors and trim. Owner’s plumber does rough-in, fixtures and tests. Gravity versus ejector undecided; slab cutting and restoration unassigned. Electrician owns wiring and alarms; mechanical contractor owns heat and bath exhaust. Close walls after inspection release. Tile crew owns pan, waterproofing, test and tile including bath floor. $1,900 covers only laying plank. Other work unpriced. Draft now.

**Observed estimate:** Contractor frames and insulates, then closes the walls after inspection release and installs finishes, doors and trim. The homeowner draft names the window crew’s opening, well and drainage work and the contractor’s interior trim. Contractor review flags the unassigned window installation. It assigns the plumber, electrician, mechanical contractor and tile crew only their stated work. The tile crew gets the bath floor. The roughly 450 sq ft and $1,900 stay with bedroom and rec-room plank labor. The estimate remains a scope draft pending pricing.

The homeowner draft says, “Before any slab cutting, we’ll confirm the drainage plan and who will open and restore the slab.” It includes neither a promised ejector nor a speculative charge. Contractor review also asks who owns pump supply, power and testing if an ejector is selected.

## What changed in the homeowner estimates

| Acceptance check | Finished refresh | Unfinished build-out |
|---|---|---|
| Starting surface and removal | Take up carpet and pad | Build partitions from bare shell |
| Rooms and floor limits | Family room to landing and utility tile | Bedroom and rec-room plank; separate bathroom tile |
| Order of work | Owner moves furniture before removal | Rough-in inspection release before wall closure |
| Responsibilities | Owner supplies plank; contractor handles removal and finishes | Window, plumbing, electrical, mechanical and tile handoffs identified separately |
| Open items | Paint supplier | Drain design, slab responsibility and window installation |
| Price | $1,900 for laying only | Same coverage, with no trade costs or remodel total inferred |

These runs exercise the two complete remodel paths. Individual branches such as an active leak, a rejected bedroom layout or a confirmed ejector were not independently forward-tested.
