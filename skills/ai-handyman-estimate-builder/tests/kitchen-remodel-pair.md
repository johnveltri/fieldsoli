# Kitchen remodel paired conversation test

Two evaluators independently used the skill and the kitchen playbook. Give each the same intake, then reveal only the answer for its branch. Compare the homeowner drafts and unresolved checks below. Dollar amounts are fixture inputs only.

**Shared intake:** “I’m doing a kitchen remodel for the Parkers. Cabinets and tops, sink and dishwasher. I have $3,600 for cabinet installation labor. Help me scope it.”

**Observed first turns:** Both asked what comes out, whether the layout stays, what the cabinet labor covers and who takes over at template and final hookup. The refresh evaluator asked what the kitchen would look like on day one. The gut evaluator asked who owns removal and haul-away.

## A. Cabinet and counter refresh

**Contractor answer:** Same layout. Protect existing floor and tile splash; remove and haul old cabinets and tops. Install nine assembled owner-supplied cabinets with fillers, ends, toe kicks and pulls. Floor/splash clearance checked. Retained dishwasher clears and side mounts fit. Owner’s stone fabricator templates, fits top and mounts sink. Contractor returns to reconnect sink and dishwasher and test both. Owner has another sink. Only cabinet install labor is priced. Draft now.

**Observed homeowner estimate, condensed:** “Scope draft — pricing to follow.” Protect the floor and backsplash, remove and haul old cabinets and tops. Install nine owner-supplied cabinets in the existing layout, level and secure them for template, and fit the agreed trim and hardware. Cabinet installation labor — $3,600. The owner’s fabricator installs the top and mounts the sink. Afterward the contractor returns to reconnect the sink, reinstall and side-mount the retained dishwasher, and test both. Removal, protection and final connection work remain unpriced.

**Contractor review:** The evaluator asked whether the new bowl and trap meet the existing drain before pricing reconnection. It kept drain rework out of the estimate. No wall opening, rough-in package, new flooring, temporary sink or general hidden-damage charge appeared.

## B. Full gut remodel

**Contractor answer:** Full gut to studs. Same nine assembled owner-supplied cabinets, trim, hardware and $3,600 installation labor. Contractor does demolition and hauling, closes and paints opened kitchen walls after rough-in release, lays new flooring and tiles the splash after counters. Sink and dishwasher move to the island. Owner’s plumber owns rough and final plumbing and tests; owner’s electrician owns island power and induction conversion; owner’s gas fitter makes the old range supply safe. Owner’s fabricator templates, installs tops and mounts sink. Retailer sets appliances. Hood route and duct responsibility undecided. Other work unpriced. Draft now.

**Observed homeowner estimate, condensed:** “Scope draft — pricing to follow.” Demolish to studs and haul debris. Close, finish and paint opened kitchen walls after rough-ins are released. Install the nine cabinets and listed trim for $3,600 labor, leaving bases ready for template. Lay new flooring and install a tile splash after countertops. The owner’s plumber connects and tests the island sink and dishwasher. The electrician, gas fitter, fabricator and retailer retain their named responsibilities. Resolve the hood route and duct owner before closing the affected walls or fitting cabinets that depend on it.

**Contractor review:** The evaluator asked whether reaching the island requires opening a finished ceiling below, what flooring and boundaries are specified, and where the splash ends. It kept ceiling repair and unassigned hood ductwork out of the contractor’s price.

## Acceptance check

| Compare the actual drafts | Observed |
|---|---|
| Layout changes removal and finish work | A protected floor and splash; B removed finishes, closed kitchen walls and included new floor and splash. |
| Hookup follows the agreed party | A included a contractor return; B assigned hookup and test to the owner's plumber and appliance placement to the retailer. |
| Known dependencies change the sequence | A waited for the fabricator before reconnection; B also waited for rough-in release before wall closure. Hood decision stayed open. |
| Price stays attached to its input | Both retained $3,600 only for installing nine cabinets; neither calculated a remodel total or priced other parties. |
| Finish dates stay specific | B grouped flooring and splash in one bullet. Its contractor review correctly left the floor order open, but the homeowner sentence could read as scheduling both after counters. The final guide now tells the writer to give each operation its own timing when this matters. |
