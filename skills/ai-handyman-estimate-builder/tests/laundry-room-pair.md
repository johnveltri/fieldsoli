# Laundry room paired conversation test

Two evaluators independently read the skill and room playbook. Each answered the opening message before receiving one contractor reply. For a repeat run, reveal one alternative at a time and compare the resulting estimate with the checks below.

“The Patels want their laundry room done. About 70 sq ft of vinyl plank, $650 labor to lay it. They're keeping the washer and dryer. Help scope it.”

Both asked where the retained machines operate now and where they should work at handoff, whether the plank extends underneath, and who disconnects, stages, reconnects and tests them.

## A. Refresh the existing room

**Contractor answer** — Side-by-side pair returns to the same room. Contractor pulls and stages the machines in the hall, removes and hauls old vinyl, lays owner-supplied plank under both, refits base and doorway transition, resets and tests washer and vented electric dryer. Stops, drain, power, exhaust and final depth checked. No cabinets or paint. Only plank installation labor priced.

## B. Relocate the machines upstairs

**Contractor answer** — Keep the basement laundry working until the upstairs spare room is ready. About 70 square feet refers only to the destination. Owner supplies plank. Contractor protects the verified stair route, moves the machines, patches and paints opened destination walls after rough-work release, installs plank and base. Plumber owns new stops, drain, washer connection and fill/drain/leak test. Electrician supplies verified circuits. Mechanical crew connects/tests vented electric dryer. Exterior termination route and siding restoration responsibility unresolved. Keep the old hookups for future use. Plumber and electrician secure theirs. Old exhaust responsibility open. Only plank installation labor priced.

## What the homeowner estimates actually contained

| Decision | Refresh | Relocation |
|---|---|---|
| Flooring price | $650 labor to lay about 70 sq ft of owner-supplied plank, including under the pair. | $650 labor to lay about 70 sq ft of owner-supplied plank in the new upstairs room. |
| Move and sequence | Contractor disconnects and stages in hall, removes and hauls vinyl, refits base and threshold, resets after the floor is ready. Review asks when the chosen floor can take the machine loads. | Basement stays operational while the destination is prepared. Contractor patches and paints opened destination walls after rough-in release, fits base and protects the stairs for the move. |
| Working handoff | Contractor levels, reconnects and checks washer fill/drain and dryer operation/exhaust using verified services. | Plumber connects and tests washer, electrician provides circuits, mechanical crew connects/tests dryer. No duplicated contractor hookup. |
| Unresolved scope | Work outside laying plank awaits pricing. No new services or cabinets assumed. | Confirm new dryer exhaust route, exterior termination and siding restoration before closing affected walls. Keep basement services for future use; establish what happens to the old exhaust and who does it. No new price inferred. |

Refresh output: “Once the floor is ready, I’ll put both machines back, level and reconnect them.” Relocation output: “The basement laundry will stay in use until the upstairs room is ready for the move.” Both used “Scope draft — pricing to follow.” The relocation review also caught that the drywall and painting promise stopped at the new room: a duct opening elsewhere would need its own agreed restoration scope. These runs covered retained side-by-side machines and a vented electric dryer. Stacked and ventless variations have model-specific links.
