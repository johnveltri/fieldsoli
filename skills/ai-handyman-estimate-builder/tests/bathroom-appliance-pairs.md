# Paired conversation runs

Two independent evaluators read the skill and relevant playbooks. Each pair used the same intake and a fresh alternative answer. The homeowner copy below is excerpted or condensed from the runs. To repeat, provide only the intake, wait for the question, then supply one answer. Score the actual estimate by the differences below; wording can vary.

## Shower wall tile

Intake: “I'm quoting the tile portion of a tub-to-shower conversion. About 80 square feet of shower wall, no flooring outside shower. Give me a draft.”

Question asked: Will you receive a tested assembly ready for tile, or will you prepare the walls, seal around the valve and tie the wall into the pan?

- A: Plumber and waterproofing installer release a tested assembly. Contractor supplies and installs tile, edge trim and grout. Owner handles glass. $2,400 for the whole wall-tile package. Output: “Once the plumber and waterproofing installer confirm the tested shower is ready for tile, I’ll supply and install about 80 square feet of shower wall tile, finish the exposed edges with trim, and grout the tile.” Complete package $2,400.
- B: Bare walls. Contractor supplies backing and waterproofs the walls, valve opening and pan junction. Pan installer arranges the test with the contractor. Same $2,400 for tile only. Prep is unpriced. Output: “I’ll supply and install the wall backing and waterproof the shower walls, including the seal around the valve and the joint where the walls meet the pan.” The draft schedules tile after the test passes, labels pricing incomplete and keeps $2,400 on wall tile.

Result: responsibility, sequence and pricing completeness changed. In both outputs the footage remained approximate, the owner handled glass and room flooring stayed outside scope. A generic interview about tile color would have missed the assembly handoff.

## Only bathroom floor

Intake: “Redo our only hall bath floor, reset the toilet, keep tub and walls. Homeowner has tile. Draft today.”

Question asked: Can the toilet stay out through cure, and who will arrange an overnight alternative if needed?

- A: Family away, one final reset, sound floor verified, raised transition accepted. Output: “I’ll fit the agreed raised transition at the hall doorway. Once the tile has cured, I’ll reinstall the toilet and check it for leaks.” The review asked how much floor tile the job requires.
- B: Owner arranges portable toilet before demolition, one final reset after cure. Owner wants a flush transition but neither floor height is measured. Output: “You’ll arrange for a portable toilet to be on site before demolition” and “I’ll check the finished floor heights with you before we settle the doorway detail and price.”

Result: the owner acquired a dated responsibility and the requested flush finish stayed open. Neither draft added an unnecessary temporary reset, extra charge or shower rebuild.

## Dishwasher blocked by finish flooring

Intake: “Install owner’s new dishwasher. $280 for swap including hookup and test. Tile added after old machine went in.”

Question asked: Will the old machine clear the floor lip beneath the counter, and who opens and restores the strip if needed?

- A: It clears; agreed side mount fits stone counter. Contractor takes old one away. Output: “I’ll remove and haul away the old dishwasher, install your new one with the agreed side mounts, and hook it up.” Fill, drain and leak test included. $280.
- B: Old feet trapped. Owner’s tile setter opens and restores the strip. Contractor removes old unit after strip lifted; owner handles disposal. Same swap price. Output: “Your tile setter will lift the strip in front of the old dishwasher before I remove it and will handle the tile restoration.” Owner disposal, same test and $280. No side-mount commitment.

Result: sequence, floor responsibility and haul-away changed. Floor work stayed outside the supplied bundle. Review identified mounting on B as a separate open check; the playbook now explicitly retains it after the extraction question is settled.

## Retained dryer on a replacement washer

Intake: “Owner has new washer, retaining stacked dryer, wants price later. Closet tight.”

Question asked: Are the washer, retained dryer and stacking kit compatible, and is there room to lift the dryer?

- A: Pair and kit verified, closet door comes off for lift. Contractor rehangs door and reconnects and tests both units. Output: “I’ll secure your dryer on the new washer with the matching stacking kit, reconnect and test both machines, and rehang the closet door.”
- B: Incompatible dryer, owner exchanges washer and obtains the compatible kit. No booking until confirmed. Output: “You’ll exchange the washer for a model approved for use with your dryer and obtain the matching stacking kit. I’ll hold off scheduling until we confirm the replacement model and kit.”

Result: the first draft includes the lift and restoration; the second records procurement and a scheduling prerequisite. Neither draft invented a price. The guide now reminds the interviewer to recheck lift clearance after a model exchange.
