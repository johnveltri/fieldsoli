# Paired room addition test

Two independent evaluators used the skill and room addition interview. Give the same intake to each, then reveal only one contractor reply. Compare quantities, trade responsibilities, timing and the scope of each price in the homeowner copy.

“The Rivera family is adding onto the back of their house. About 180 sq ft of bedroom plank. My $1,250 is labor to install bedroom plank only. Help scope my bid.”

Both first turns asked which rooms and work packages the contractor owns and where the flooring price stops. One asked about subfloor readiness and materials. The other asked who repairs the existing side of the opening.

## Bedroom

**Contractor answer** — Ground-level bedroom only. Builder owns foundation, framing, roof and wall connection, window, HVAC, electrical, opening to the hall and hall-side finishes. Contractor receives a dry shell after rough-work release, drywalls and paints bedroom walls and ceiling, fits the door/base and installs owner-supplied plank on a verified ready subfloor to the matching-height hall threshold. Other contractor work is unpriced.

## Primary suite

**Contractor answer** — Builder provides dry shell and owns foundation, framing, flashing and house opening. Contractor patches and paints the existing hall wall to its corners, closes walls after rough-work release, paints suite, fits doors/base and lays owner-supplied plank in the bedroom and walk-in closet. Only bedroom plank installation has a price. Plumber owns rough/final connections and testing; bath waste route past an existing beam remains unverified. Bath installer owns waterproofing, test and tile. Glass crew measures after tile. Closet company supplies and installs custom drawers and rods; contractor installs blocking and awaits locations. Electrician and HVAC contractor own their trades.

## Observed homeowner estimates

| Check | Bedroom run | Primary suite run |
|---|---|---|
| Existing house | Builder finishes the hall side. Contractor stops bedroom plank at the matching-height threshold. | Contractor patches the affected existing hall wall and paints to its corners. Builder owns the opening and flashing. |
| Bedroom price | “Bedroom plank installation labor — $1,250.” About 180 sq ft of owner-supplied plank. Other interior finish work awaits pricing. | Same $1,250 and approximate bedroom area. The homeowner draft adds a separate unpriced closet floor line. No invented square footage or price for the extension. |
| Bathroom | No bath lines, service relocation or speculative charge. | Plumber verifies waste route past beam before closure. Bath installer waterproofs, tests and tiles. Glass crew measures after tile. No beam alteration promised. |
| Closet | No closet line. | Contractor installs blocking before walls close. Closet company supplies and fits drawers and rods. Review asks for backing locations and a separate closet floor measurement. |
| Handoff | Dry shell and released concealed work before bedroom finishing. Review asks who supplies door, board, paint and base, and whether door is slab or prehung. | Dry shell plus trade releases. Review asks who opens and restores any surface affected by the revised drain route and who fits the floor transitions. |

Both results were labeled “Scope draft — pricing to follow.” All five comparison checks passed. These two runs exercised a finish contractor’s bid on ground-level additions. The shell-construction and second-story questions are indexed but were not independently exercised in this pair.
