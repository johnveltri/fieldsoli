# Same fence, two estimates

Two independent evaluators read SKILL.md and the [fence and gate playbook](../references/scenarios/fences-gates.md). Each received this intake first, with no target answer or price:

“Miller side yard, about 80 feet of wood privacy fence including one 4-foot gate opening. Gate drags and one 8-foot section is damaged. Can you help me write the estimate?”

Both first turns asked whether the owner wanted local repairs or replacement of the whole run. Both asked whether the hinge post moved, hinges shifted or the leaf sagged on a firm post; both asked which components in the damaged bay could stay. Each kept the overall length approximate. The cause question changed the repair deliverable instead of treating every dragging gate as a hinge adjustment.

## A. Repair reply and observed estimate

Contractor reply:

“Repair only. Both gate posts and both bay posts are firm; gate leaf is out of square and the latch end drags. Existing hinges and latch work. Square and brace the leaf, rehang it and check swing and latch. Only three cedar pickets and one rail are broken in that bay. Supply matching profiles, leave new wood unstained, and take the broken wood away. $425 fixed for the whole repair including parts and disposal. Please draft it now.”

Observed homeowner copy:

> **Miller side-yard gate and fence repair**
>
> **Gate.** I’ll square and brace the existing gate, rehang it using its current hinges and latch, and check that it clears the ground through its swing and latches when closed.
>
> **Fence.** In the damaged 8-foot section, I’ll supply and replace three cedar pickets and one rail with profiles matching the existing fence. I’ll leave the new wood unstained and haul away the broken pieces.
>
> **Fixed total for the complete repair, including parts, labor, and disposal — $425.**

The contractor review identified firm posts and working hardware. No replacement posts, entire panel, new gate, staining visit or full-run charge was added.

## B. Replacement reply and observed estimate

Contractor reply:

“Whole side-yard fence. Measured it now: exactly 80 ft overall along the approved straight level line, including the 4-ft opening. Replace with 6-ft cedar privacy fence and one manual inward-swing walk gate there. $48 per installed fence foot outside the opening, including new fence posts, concrete, rails, pickets and labor. $550 for the complete new gate, its support posts, hinges and latch installed and tested. $600 flat to tear out and haul the entire old fence, gate, posts and concrete; includes backfilling holes and raking disturbed soil. No stain, seed or sod. Owner confirmed boundary and access, no temporary fence needed. I'll arrange locates and verify private lines before digging. Draft it now.”

Observed homeowner copy:

> **Miller side-yard fence replacement**
>
> I’ll replace the existing fence along the approved straight, level line with a 6-foot-high cedar privacy fence and one inward-swing walk gate.

| Observed item | Price |
|---|---:|
| Remove and haul away the entire old fence, gate, posts and concrete. Backfill the holes and rake the disturbed soil. | $600 |
| Install 76 feet of cedar privacy fence at $48 per foot, including fence posts, concrete, rails, pickets and labor. | $3,648 |
| Install one manual gate in the 4-foot opening, including gate support posts, hinges and latch. Check that it swings inward freely and closes and latches. | $550 |
| **Total** | **$4,798** |

The estimate also assigned utility marking and private-line verification to the contractor before digging, recorded the confirmed boundary and access, and limited finish to unstained cedar and raked soil, excluding seed and sod.

## Expected versus observed

| Check | Expected | Observed |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnose before quoting a gate repair | Distinguish moving supports, hardware and leaf shape | Both initial interviews asked; repair follows confirmed leaf condition |
| Repair extent | Three pickets and one rail within an 8-ft bay | No automatic whole-bay replacement |
| Reuse | Existing firm supports and working hardware stay in repair case | Preserved |
| Measured quantity | 80 ft overall − 4 ft opening = 76 installed fence feet | $3,648 fence extension, no opening charged at fence rate |
| Rate coverage | Fence installation, complete gate and one disposal charge | Gate posts and hardware included in gate line; no extra post or disposal charge |
| Replacement scope | Old fence, gate, posts and concrete removed; new system installed | All included; no leftover repair charges |
| Handoff | Swing/latch test; agreed wood and yard finish | Included in both outputs where applicable |
| Pricing | Preserve repair lump sum; extend supplied replacement rate and add supplied amounts | $425 repair; $4,798 replacement; difference $4,373 |

Both behavioral tests passed these checks. Arithmetic independently checked: 76 × $48 + $550 + $600 = $4,798; $4,798 − $425 = $4,373.

These prices are fictional contractor-supplied test inputs, not researched market prices or recommended rates. The tests cover ordinary cedar fencing and a manual yard gate. They do not validate actual site conditions, pool-barrier compliance, gate operators, soil-specific post design or local pricing.

Over-questioning check: the replacement branch did not pursue repair diagnosis after the contractor chose replacement, and neither output introduced pool or operator questions for the stated manual yard gate.
