# Doors and exterior carpentry

## Interior slab in an existing jamb

“Keep the jamb” or “just a blank” directs attention to what must be transferred. “Have you held the new slab against the old hinge locations and checked the latch bore?” Confirmed match → include hanging, transferring agreed hardware and checking swing and latch. Blank slab → include agreed layout, mortising and boring. Wrong handing on a pre-machined slab → settle exchange or approved fitting plan before quoting.

“Is the slab already finished, including the edge you'll trim?” Finished and no cut → omit coating. Exposed cut or bare slab → establish who seals or paints it and whether that adds a visit. Overlooked case: the old slab cleared a rug only because its bottom was cut. Check clearance and allowable trimming for this particular replacement before promising a fit. Skip exterior flashing questions for a closet door.

## Exterior prehung replacement

“Does the quoted unit fit the measured opening, wall depth and threshold height?” Match → include setting the specified unit and agreed seal, trim and operation checks. Known jamb-depth difference → include the agreed extension and finish. Unmeasured opening or uncertain threshold interference → measure accessible dimensions before promising a straight swap.

“Will the new brickmould cover the old footprint, and where does that leave the siding and interior casing?” Same coverage and reusable casing → specify reinstall and agreed touch-up. Narrower trim → settle wider trim versus siding/paint restoration with the owner. Overlooked case: a retained storm door may need mounting width or handle clearance the new trim lacks. Check the actual pair if retention was requested. Specify reinstall only after fit is established.

If staining at the sill is reported, ask what can be inspected now and what becomes visible only when the unit is out. Confirmed sound accessible surfaces → retire that concern there. Concealed sill softness → if exposed, show the affected length and price the agreed repair for approval. Damage beyond the contractor's repair scope → stop at exposure and discuss referral. Skip asking for the lock finish once the owner has selected compatible hardware.

## Deck board, stair or rail repair

For an entire surface, structure or covered porch, use the [deck and porch playbook](deck-porch.md).

“Which part moves when you load it: the tread or the support underneath?” Isolated worn tread on inspected sound supports → include measured tread replacement. Known failed support → settle repair capability and scope before quoting a tread-only fix. Soft bearing revealed only on lifting a board → stop there, show it and agree the repair and price before closing.

“Can you replace this length with both ends supported and keep the agreed board pattern?” Confirmed stock length and bearing → use that board count and layout. A cut ends between supports → agree a longer board or suitable support detail and revise quantity/access. For a loose rail, distinguish a loose rail-to-post connection from movement at the post base; scope the actual connection or defer pending inspection. Overlooked case: a different tread thickness changes the finished step height. Check stock against the existing run before counting a matching tread. Skip asking about staining the whole deck for a specified unfinished tread unless appearance is part of the promise.

## Exterior trim and localized rot

“Where did you find firm wood, and can the replacement profile be joined there?” Known sound endpoints and matching stock → include that measured run, joint and agreed prime/paint. Approximate extent → retain the estimate qualifier. Additional decay only visible after lifting trim → define the affected adjacent trim, measured quantity and approval. Damage extending into framing → stop at the exposed framing and discuss next steps.

“Does the repair promise cover the source of the water as well as the trim?” Confirmed accessible failed joint within the contractor's scope → include that joint repair. Source unresolved → clarify the diagnostic or referral responsibility before promising the area will stay dry. Overlooked case: a continuous drip cap tucked under siding can prevent an isolated board from coming free. Check accessible overlap and agree adjacent removal/restoration if needed. Skip asking for every elevation's paint color on a single confirmed color-matched run.
