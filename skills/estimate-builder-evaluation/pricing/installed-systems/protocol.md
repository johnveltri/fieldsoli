# MassCEC residential ASHP comparison

Frozen before reading any outcome cells. Dataset and header inspected. Use the one agency-published residential ASHP sheet, nominal U.S. dollars and Massachusetts project-state records only. Require a valid installation date, program Date Paid, positive reported Total Costs and positive integer outdoor-unit count. Discard records whose payment precedes installation, and dates beyond the download. Program payment is distinct from a household's payment to an installer.

Dedupe conservatively by grouping on normalized project street, town and installation date, retaining one row for matching outcome and model inputs and omitting ambiguous groups rather than summing. Require a usable address and town for this. No personal names, street addresses or project-level IDs in outputs. Choose an installation-date split after the first 80% of eligible rows in chronological order, moving all records on the boundary date to holdout. To avoid predicting another job at a known site, remove from the held-out set every address/town seen in training. No random shuffle, inflation adjustments, geographic calibration or price trimming. Record counts and dates by split.

Primary comparison: predict training median Total Costs for everyone vs training median by number of outdoor units (1, 2, 3+). Fall back to global for groups with fewer than 20 training observations. An additional model refines that count by total indoor units (1, 2, 3, 4+) for integer positive inputs and training groups of at least 20; otherwise fall back to outdoor model. Prespecified dollars before rebates, absolute errors, MAE, median absolute error, mean signed bias, fraction within 25% of reported cost, and coverage of empirical central 80% training intervals using identical group fallbacks. Show distribution by outdoor count and compare errors without pooling families across different datasets. Mark which models win or regress. Scope is historical program-rebated installations, with no test for labor-only repairs or today's current quotes. Proportions and records counts alone cannot establish invoice accuracy or the impact of contractor experience.

Before reading costs, inspection of dates alone showed invalid entries including zero and obvious pre-program dates. Restrict both analysis dates to November 1, 2014 or later (program launch month). Agency program closure in March 2019 limits applications, not necessarily installation/payment dates; retain later completed installations up to the snapshot. Check time leakage by admitting to training only projects with a payment/record completion date strictly before the first held-out installation date. Report delayed-payment exclusions separately.
