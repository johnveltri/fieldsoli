# Prospective multi-city test: real household work

1. Two researchers select development and held-out projects separately. The pricing evaluator receives only the year, location, work and responsibility for materials, with money, job source/provider, identifying details and price-bearing links withheld. The held-out researcher is asked to avoid CostHelper, Chicago-replay providers and prior calibration cases. Do not count two invoices from one job as two projects. Look for at least two priced components and evidence of completed work/payment; a reported payment is not an audited receipt. Record scope, provenance, component reconciliation, cities, periods and any eligibility exclusions.
2. Price all scopes with the archived current skill before disclosing any paid values to the pricing evaluator. Do not search customer stories or look up the projects. Use the skill's offline references; independent price research for marketplace rate cards / historical data may be added to the skill by the developer. Predict low, central and high in nominal USD for the job date, distinguishing package vs billed components and contractor-supplied vs separately purchased amounts. Missing facts may justify abstention. A range needs an explicit central estimate to enter point comparisons. Record assumptions, market/period limitations and numeric coverage.
3. Inspect the development payments only after freezing initial forecasts. Diagnose the largest dollar and percentage misses, checking for accounting, geography, time and trade/channel mismatches before changing the skill. Freeze revised instructions/data and re-estimate the untouched test scopes, with their paid figures still sealed. Do not use an across-the-board factor fitted to these jobs. Preserve any failures on the fresh jobs.
4. Compare the original and revised predictions for the *same* held-out projects after both forecasts are frozen. Report MAE (dollars), median absolute percentage error, signed bias, counts within 20%, range coverage and widths, abstentions. Summarize development separately. Numbers from the same source/site, region or vintage may be correlated. Do not claim broad accuracy from a small sample; do not move an outlier to development, or drop an uncommon price because it looks expensive. If the estimate deliberately excludes a receipt line, report raw total and reconcile it before scoring; do not mix a partial subtotal with a full payment. Note any departures and their timing.

## Follow-up after opening the first holdout

Preserve the first holdout and its scores unchanged. Freeze a final candidate with narrower rules about uncertain second-task time, unmeasured chimney/plaster/exterior work and lifetime warranties. Seek an additional cohort of jobs explicitly involving a handyman. Keep its payments sealed through both passes (original stock versus final candidate); compare like-for-like indicative planning numbers from the same estimating agent using identical scope assumptions. Separately record an independent reading of the stock skill's willingness to issue a complete price. A refusal to quote is neither a zero-dollar estimate nor a price hit. The additional cohort is reported separately, and never used to refit a method and rescore itself.

The source extractors necessarily read prices to verify eligibility and settlement; they did not produce the estimates. The developer could see anonymized scope descriptions to research independent service menus. This is a price-blind replay, not a double-blind trial.
